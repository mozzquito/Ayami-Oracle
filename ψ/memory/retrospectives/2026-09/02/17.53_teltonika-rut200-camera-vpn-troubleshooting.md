## Lessons Learned

- When a device is reachable through a VPN gateway's own admin UI but a device *behind* that gateway is not, check the gateway's inter-zone **Forwarding** policy before anything else — Input-chain (traffic terminating at the gateway) and Forwarding-chain (traffic passing through to LAN) are independently configured on OpenWrt/RUTOS-family firewalls, and a permissive Input rule tells you nothing about Forwarding.
- On RUTOS (and OpenWrt generally), a newly-added WireGuard road-warrior interface commonly gets its own firewall zone with **Forwarding to LAN defaulted to Reject** — this is a very common root cause for "VPN connects fine, but nothing behind the router is reachable," and is worth checking early, not late.
- DHCP lease tables on OpenWrt/RUTOS live in `/tmp` (tmpfs) and are wiped by any reboot — an empty or incomplete lease table right after a reboot is not evidence of a device's IP-assignment mode; check lease age/freshness before concluding a device is statically configured.
- MAC address OUI prefixes (the first 3 octets) are a fast, no-tooling way to shortlist candidate devices of a known vendor inside a large ARP/lease table when the device's hostname field is empty.
- A WireGuard client's **Allowed IPs** must include the full destination subnet (not just the peer's tunnel `/32`) for LAN-side devices to be reachable — router-side firewall fixes alone won't matter if the client never routes that traffic into the tunnel in the first place.
